Families often ask whether a hospital journey should be made in an ambulance or a wheelchair-accessible cab. The answer depends on the passenger's medical needs, not simply on the fact that the destination is a hospital.

## When to arrange an ambulance

An ambulance is the appropriate option when a passenger needs urgent medical attention, monitoring, oxygen, clinical assistance, or other care during the journey. In an emergency, contact local emergency services or the passenger's healthcare provider. Do not use a taxi in place of emergency response or medical transport.

## When a wheelchair taxi may be appropriate

A wheelchair taxi is for planned, non-emergency transportation. It may suit a stable passenger travelling to a scheduled consultation, a follow-up, a diagnostic appointment, dialysis, or a planned discharge when no medical monitoring is required en route. A wheelchair-accessible vehicle can help avoid an unsuitable standard cab when boarding and wheelchair fit are important.

Every passenger and chair is different. Before booking, confirm that the vehicle can accommodate the wheelchair, whether the passenger will remain seated in it, and how the boarding process works. Share wheelchair dimensions or type if known, along with the number of caregivers who will travel.

## Questions to ask before you confirm

- Is the passenger stable and able to travel without medical supervision?
- Has the healthcare team advised what kind of transport is suitable?
- Can the cab accommodate the passenger's wheelchair and mobility aids?
- Does the journey need a return pickup, waiting time, or a second trip?
- What is the complete fare for the route and requested arrangements?
- What pickup point and hospital entrance should the driver use?

If any medical condition makes the trip uncertain, ask the treating clinician before choosing a taxi. A transport provider cannot assess a passenger's medical fitness to travel.
