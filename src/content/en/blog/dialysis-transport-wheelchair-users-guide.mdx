When a person uses a wheelchair and attends dialysis on a regular schedule, transport is part of the care routine. A suitable ride should be planned around appointment time, the passenger's mobility needs, and what happens after treatment. This guide helps families prepare a clear request for a non-emergency wheelchair taxi.

## First decide whether a taxi is appropriate

A wheelchair-accessible taxi is for transportation. It is not an ambulance and does not provide clinical support during a journey. If the passenger is medically unstable, needs oxygen or monitoring, or may need urgent care on the way, contact the healthcare team about appropriate medical transport.

For a passenger who is stable and does not need medical monitoring in transit, a wheelchair cab may be an option for a planned dialysis appointment. Ask the treating care team if you are unsure which kind of transport is suitable.

## Share the full journey plan

Before booking, gather the information that helps the cab provider check the trip:

1. Passenger's full pickup address and the clinic or hospital destination.
2. Appointment date and check-in time, plus any guidance from the clinic about arrival.
3. Wheelchair type and approximate size; say whether the passenger stays seated in it.
4. Number of caregivers or family members travelling.
5. Whether you need a one-way ride, a return pickup, a later second trip, or waiting.
6. Mobility aids or luggage that will also travel.

Ask the booking team to confirm the vehicle is suitable for the wheelchair and the companion count. A powered wheelchair, a large chair, or a different boarding arrangement may require specific confirmation.

## Make a practical return-trip arrangement

Dialysis appointment end times may not always match the original estimate. Before travelling, agree on how the return journey will work: a scheduled pickup, a call when the passenger is ready, or a confirmed waiting arrangement. Do not assume that a driver can wait or that a return vehicle will be immediately available unless it has been confirmed.

For repeat visits, ask whether the dates can be scheduled in advance. Keep the pickup location, destination, appointment time, and contact details consistent in each request, and let the provider know promptly when the clinic changes a session.

## Keep the passenger comfortable and informed

On the day, have the booking confirmation and phone number available. Allow time for pickup, boarding, and the drive, particularly when the appointment has a fixed check-in time. Let the driver know about access instructions such as a building entrance or a safe pickup point.

Caregivers can help by checking that the passenger's personal items and mobility aids are ready before the vehicle arrives. Any transfer or boarding assistance should be discussed in advance; do not assume a driver can provide clinical lifting or personal care.
